## Month 2 — `minisklearn`: From Distributions to Models (2026-08-24 → )

**What Month 2 builds:** `minisklearn` — a small, importable Python package (`linear_model.py`, `tree.py`, `ensemble.py`, `cluster.py`, `metrics.py`) exposing every Week-3 algorithm behind a consistent `.fit(X, y)` / `.predict(X)` API, plus week-1/2 utilities (Naive Bayes spam filter, entropy-based information gain, MLE/MAP fitters, CLT demo). Week 4 validates the whole thing against real `sklearn` on a real Kaggle dataset and ships it with a blog post and a public GitHub repo.

**How it builds on Month 1:**
Month 1 gave you the calculus and optimisation substrate — the ability to differentiate through any computation graph and to train via gradient descent (your autograd engine → MLP → Adam). Month 2 reuses that substrate directly: its linear and logistic regression are *gradient-descent-trained* learners, so you can even swap in your Month-1 Adam instead of vanilla SGD for a continuity flex (the plan explicitly calls this out on Week 3 Day 1). Information-theoretic loss functions (cross-entropy, BCE — the Week 2 core) are now the objectives that autograd would differentiate, and the covariance/PCA intuition from Month 1 returns in Week 1 Day 5's covariance matrix and Week 3 Day 6's K-Means geometry. In short: Month 1 built the engine; Month 2 builds the first real cars that run on it, and proves they steer correctly by matching `sklearn`'s answers.
